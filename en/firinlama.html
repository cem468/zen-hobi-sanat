Kiln & Process

From clay to a lasting piece: what happens inside the kiln?

Every piece our students make is fired in our own kiln, without ever leaving the studio. Before it dries and meets the fire, ceramic clay is fragile; to become permanent, water-resistant and fit for everyday use, each piece goes through two separate firings: first a bisque firing, then a glaze firing after glazing. We manage this two-stage process for you with care, in our own electric kiln.

  • Every piece is fired in our studio's own kiln
  • Every ceramic piece is fired twice in total: bisque + glaze
  • Bisque firing: approximately 900–980°C
  • Glaze firing: approximately 1000–1060°C
  • Heating, holding and slow cooling are all carefully controlled
  • Kiln rental available for ceramics made outside the studio

Step by Step

From the kiln to your home

Every ceramic piece takes its final, permanent form after being fired twice in total in our own kiln.

1. Drying

A shaped piece must dry completely before it goes into the kiln. Since a damp piece can crack inside the kiln, drying is done patiently, over several days.

2. Bisque Firing

Once dry, the clay is fired for the first time at approximately 900–980°C. This firing turns the clay into a porous yet durable "bisque" — now ready to be glazed.

3. Glazing & Glaze Firing

The glazed piece is fired a second time, generally at 1000–1060°C. At this temperature, the glaze turns into glass, giving the surface a glossy, waterproof and colourful finish.
